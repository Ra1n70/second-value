THE RULES

Buy with a reason.
Know when to walk away.

  1. Everyone starts a new session with 1,000 virtual coins once. Cash, profits and losses carry between auctions for every player.
  2. Each crate contains six objects. Everyone shares ordinary sale prices, fixed before bidding.
  3. New sessions: Each crate has a public source. The source changes draw probabilities, not guaranteed contents. Identities and ordinary prices are fixed before bidding.
  4. After settlement, compare the buyer’s actual result with other players’ hypothetical returns at the same winning bid. Fees and available cash are included; these comparisons do not change anyone’s balance.
  5. New sessions: The auction house publishes a reserve before bidding, around 10% of the crate’s ordinary sale value (8–12%). Role abilities do not affect it. It stays fixed for all three rounds; even a sole bid must meet it. If the crate goes unsold, nobody pays.
  6. Your role and collection theme stay locked for the session. Ability uses reset only when a new crate opens.
  7. The Appraiser may identify two objects per crate across its three rounds. Known and public information is free to read.
  8. The Restorer may choose up to two repairs per crate after winning. Pay fees first; each net gain is capped at 25% of base value and 60 coins.
  9. The Collector may deliver one genuine pair per crate with different identities and categories in the locked theme. Bonus is 25% of the pair’s base values, up to 120.
  10. The Salvager sells up to two reproductions as parts. Each sale is 90 with a 15 fee; compare this with ordinary value.
  11. The Curator exhibits exactly three genuine, intact objects from different categories. Bonus is 20% per object, up to 40 each, with one 25 fee.
  12. The Antique Dealer sells one genuine, intact object to a regular customer for a 30% premium, up to 90. Packing and delivery cost 25, so low-value sales may lose money.
  13. Round 1 starts with silhouettes. Round 2 reveals the genuine-object count. Round 3 reveals one object’s full identity and the repairable count.
  14. Early sale needs at least 1.8× the runner-up in round 1, or 1.3× in round 2. The highest bid wins round 3.
  15. Unsuccessful rounds reveal all bids and charge nothing. You may change or skip each round’s bid, up to your current cash.
  16. Before reveal, return to clues or withdraw the bid review to edit your amount. Used inspections stay used. Once revealed, bids and passes are final for that round.
  17. Ties follow the priority order shown before bidding.
  18. Finish selling the crate before continuing or leaving. All objects are sold; no inventory carries over yet.
  19. Leave after any settled auction to finalize cumulative cash and rankings. Leaving grants no refund or extra payout.

Only the final buyer pays once. Computer quotes are fixed before your current bid and never exceed that computer’s own cash.

Solo practice uses computer bidders. Trading, item storage and a timer are not included.

Refreshing restores your session when browser storage is available.
